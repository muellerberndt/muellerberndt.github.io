Actual brains. A continuous stream of consciousness.

Mission

We are building artificial intelligence that works like animal and human brains. Local, continuous, plastic for its whole life, and able to repair itself. That is the road to general intelligence, and it is the same mathematics that produces the universe.

Why transformers are a dead end

Scaling a frozen network does not make a mind.

A transformer is trained in a regime no animal is ever in: a static corpus in shuffled batches, a global gradient through a stored graph, then a freeze. After that it never learns again. The industry's answer is to scale it. Each tenfold increase in compute buys a few benchmark points, the internet's text is spent, and the one thing that would make the system intelligent, learning from its own life, is absent by construction. You cannot scale your way out of an architecture that cannot remember what happened to it this morning.

  • Train, freeze, deployLearning stops the day the model ships. Everything it meets in production is forgotten when the context window scrolls. No brain works that way.
  • A global gradient through a stored graphBackpropagation needs every layer's activity kept for the backward pass and a global error routed to every weight. No neuron does that, and it is why training costs what it does.
  • Replay instead of memoryLearning anything new means shuffled batches and replay buffers, because learning in the order life arrives makes it forget. Memory is bolted on outside the network.
  • Imagined and witnessed are the same thingA transformer has no distinction between what it generated and what it observed. That is where hallucination comes from, and no amount of scale removes it.
How Cadence solves it

One brain. One mode. One life.

A Cadence brain is made of cortices that settle together into an equilibrium, where a transformer stacks feedforward layers. Each synapse changes from the activity of its own two neurons. Memory is written into the records the current reading touches, one witnessed outcome at a time. The same brain acts and learns from the moment it starts until it stops.

The design comes straight from our physics. An observer patch in Observer Patch Holography keeps records, reads its neighbours and repairs disagreement until the net settles. A Cadence cortex is that patch in software, and a thought is the state the brain settles into.

Read the quickstart → All docs
  • Learning never stopsOne ongoing loop. Each step takes in an observation and the outcome of the last action, updates memories and synapses, and issues the next action. Observations arrive while the brain is thinking, and thoughts linger.
  • Local learning, no backward passEvery synapse reads the activity of its own two neurons and one broadcast error. No computation graph is stored, so a brain the size of a connectome learns on ordinary hardware.
  • Memory from one passA sparse reading touches few records, and the witnessed outcome is written into exactly those. One stream in the order it arrives, no replay ring, no shuffled batches.
  • Imagined futures stay imaginedThe brain settles each imagined future in isolation, compares the recorded consequences and acts on the best one. An imagined outcome never becomes a witnessed fact.
Live settlementNeurons relax to rest. Every few seconds a new input arrives and they settle again. The rest state is the thought.
Embodied AGI

A robot that learns its job on the job.

The datacenter model of AI puts a frozen network behind an API and streams the robot's senses to it. A Cadence brain sits inside the machine. It learns the body it has from what its motors do, keeps learning as the body wears and the task changes, and decides in tens of milliseconds on a CPU. No round trip, no retraining run, no fleet-wide update to teach one robot one new thing.

Small local brain

Runs in a browser tab

Every brain on this page runs its whole life in JavaScript on your machine, decision for decision equal to the Python agent. Decision latency p95 is 52 ms for Connect Four and 75 ms for the artist. A robot controller needs no datacenter.

Learns the body it has

Motor babbling to competence

The arm learns its own kinematics from random movement and reaches 99% of held-out targets from one pass over its experience. Nobody wrote the body into it.

Adapts without a reset

Wear, damage, new tools

Lengthen the arm's upper link mid-life and it recovers to 99% with no reset, then returns to 99% on the original body. The artist keeps drawing when its body and its canvas change mid-drawing.

From four lives in a tab to a fleet of bodies.

The same loop that runs the arm runs a warehouse picker, an inspection drone or a surgical assistant: observe, remember, predict, act, learn from what followed. Each machine learns its own job, and each keeps its brain.

Live in the browser

Four brains you can open and watch.

Each keeps learning while you interact with it, with the whole brain rendered live beside the task, neuron by neuron. Each ships its receipt and the verifier that recomputes it.

01 · Arm

A body learned from babbling.

A two-link arm moves at random and learns its own body from what follows each motor command. To copy what you draw, it searches over the consequences it has recorded and takes the command whose predicted displacement follows the line.

MeasuredHeld-out reaching 99%; the online MLP on the same stream without replay 2%; the online transformer 40%.

A world that must be remembered.

A 6 by 6 world of walls, doors, objects, colours and words, seen one cell at a time. One life learns what follows its actions, remembers where it saw an object, corrects that memory when the object moves, holds a cue across a delay and grounds words in objects.

03 · Connect Four

The rules, learned by watching.

One life learns what a dropped stone does and which windows of four cells are completed lines from the games it plays, and chooses its moves by searching over the boards it imagines from those records. No rules, no terminal oracle, no minimax labels reach the brain. Play against it while it keeps learning from your game.

A figure shown, a figure drawn.

Get started

Install it in one line.

One line takes the library from its repository. NumPy is the only required dependency, and optional backends add Numba for fused CPU kernels, PyTorch for CUDA, and MLX for Apple silicon. Python 3.11 or newer, MIT licensed, fully typed.

GitHub → PyPI Examples on GitHub 
# install the library
$ pip install git+https://github.com/muellerberndt/cadence

import cadence as cd

brain = cd.GenericBrain.build(2, 3, hidden=16, seed=0)
action = brain.step([[position, goal]])   # no outcome yet

while alive:
    position, reward = world.apply(action)
    action = brain.step([[position, goal]], reward=[reward])

# observe, remember, predict, act, learn from the outcome
