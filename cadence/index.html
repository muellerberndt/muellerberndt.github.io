Machines that learn the way brains do.

Cadence builds a brain together with its learning life. One ongoing loop takes in an observation and the outcome of the last action, writes what it witnessed into its records, settles its regions into a joint state and chooses what to do next. The same brain acts and learns for as long as it runs.

Mission

We are building artificial intelligence that works like animal and human brains. Local, continuous, plastic for its whole life, and able to repair itself. We think that is the road to general intelligence.

The loop

Observe, remember, predict, act, learn.

Every Cadence brain lives in one mode. Each step takes in the current observation and the outcome of the previous action, updates its memories and its synapses, and issues the next action. There is one mode. No switch separates learning from use, and no point arrives at which learning has to stop.

  1. 01ObserveA reading arrives: the sensor fields, a flag for each field the senses left out, the action just taken and the reads of the brain's stores.
  2. 02RememberThe reading selects the cells that will hold its outcome, and the stores keep the witnessed evidence the application declares.
  3. 03PredictAn imagined consequence is a read of the records, so a supplied search can compare many candidate actions at the cost of a vector product each.
  4. 04ActThe brain issues one action or one word and holds it pending. A clock tick alone is not feedback, so the pending action waits for its real outcome.
  5. 05Learn from the outcomeThe witnessed outcome is written into exactly the records that reading touched, and the settled regions change their synapses from their own neurons' activity and one broadcast error.

Thinking reads the current state. Observations, rewards and corrections are what change the memories and the synapses, and an imagined outcome never becomes a witnessed fact. The application decides when each event arrives.

Memory and settling

Records hold what followed. Settling completes it.

Two kinds of memory sit side by side. A records cortex learns what follows a reading with one read and one write per outcome. The settled regions hold the work that needs a fixed point.

Records cortex

What followed this reading

  • A mean-free reading. Each input unit's running mean is subtracted, so the code follows what deviates from the usual reading and a small cue can move it.
  • A fixed sparse expansion. The reading maps onto many cells, the cells with the strongest drive keep it, the rest are inhibited to zero, and the expansion is never learned.
  • One record table per predicted field. The prediction is the activity-weighted sum of the records the reading touches, and the witnessed outcome is written into exactly those.
  • Two rates. A slow rate for consequences, so a regularity averages over many outcomes, and a fast rate for reward, so one exposure writes.
  • One stream in its own order, with no replay ring. Reading and writing a record need no settling.
Settled regions

What the fixed point is for

  • Completion. A reading that arrives in part settles into a state consistent with the rest of the brain.
  • Context across a delay. A cue seen earlier is carried in the settled state until the decision that needs it.
  • The policy, where credit arrives after other decisions have already been made. Eligibility traces hold the decision until its reward lands.
  • Every neuron reads its own potential, its synaptic input and its drive, and moves toward their sum. Transport follows the declared synapses.
  • A residual reports the remaining equation error, so a step cap stays a work limit and the fixed-point equations decide.
Live settlementUnits relax to rest. Every few seconds a new input arrives and they settle again.

Evidence

Every example ships its receipt.

A run binds its outcomes to the code and the logs that produced them. The claim and the means to check it travel together.

01

Predicates

The receipt lists each acceptance predicate with its value, its threshold, how it was aggregated over seeds and whether it passed.

02

Event logs

Every decision and outcome of the life is written to an event log. The receipt carries the sha256 of each log, the library's commit and the hashes of the stage sources.

03

An independent verifier

A separate program reads the event logs and recomputes the receipt from them. Running it is how a reader checks the run without taking our word for it.

Examples

Two lives you can open and watch.

Each one runs the loop above. Both learn from one stream with no replay ring, the world model in each is a records cortex, and each ships a page that continues a life in the browser, an acceptance receipt and the verifier that recomputes it.

01 · Arm

A body learned from babbling.

A two-link arm moves at random and learns its own body from what follows each motor command. To copy what the visitor draws, it searches over the consequences it has recorded and takes the command whose predicted displacement follows the line.

  • The forward model is a records cortex, written one witnessed transition at a time.
  • A longer upper link changes the body. The arm adapts to it with no reset, then returns to its original body.
  • The page continues one life in the browser with the whole brain live beside the arm.

MeasuredHeld-out reaching 99%; the online MLP on the same stream without replay 3%.

02 · World

A world that must be remembered.

A 6 by 6 world of walls, doors, objects, colours and words, seen one cell at a time. One life learns what follows its actions, remembers where it saw an object, corrects that memory when the object moves in view, holds a cue across a delay and grounds words in objects.

  • A request names an object and begins in a distant cell under a deadline, so a remembered place is what makes it reachable in time.
  • Four stores keep witnessed evidence: object to place, cell to its passages, the last word seen, and word to referent.
  • Controls run beside the life: erased place records, shuffled action pairing, a reward-shifted cue, and the same architecture frozen from birth.

MeasuredRequests fulfilled 100% with memory, 18% with the memory erased.

Two regimes

A corpus and a freeze, or one ongoing stream.

A transformer is trained in a regime no animal is ever in: a static corpus in shuffled batches, a global gradient, a freeze, then a window of recent tokens as the only memory. Cadence is built for the other regime. Every cell below describes a mechanism.

Scope

What the library supplies.

Cadence supplies the mechanism. The application supplies the world, its rules and the curriculum that a brain lives through.

In the library

Mechanism

Neurons, synapses, declared ports, a records cortex, settled regions with checked convergence, reward eligibility, evolution over short lives, and receipts. Real connectomes load as plain data.

In the application

Goals and language

Learned goals and language are wiring an application writes. Cadence makes no claim to reproduce human learning and ships no pretrained chatbot.

On the reader

Residuals and cost

Check the equation residuals before calling a state an equilibrium, and measure task quality beside learning cost. Local updates on their own guarantee neither capability nor speed.

Get started

Install it in one line.

One line takes the library from its repository. NumPy is the only required dependency, and optional backends add Numba for fused CPU kernels, PyTorch for CUDA, and MLX for Apple silicon. Python 3.11 or newer, MIT licensed, fully typed.

import cadence as cd

brain = cd.GenericBrain.build(2, 3, hidden=16, seed=0)
action = brain.step([[position, goal]])   # no outcome yet

while alive:
    position, reward = world.apply(action)
    action = brain.step([[position, goal]], reward=[reward])
