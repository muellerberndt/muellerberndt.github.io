Mission

We are building artificial intelligence that works like animal and human brains. Local, continuous, plastic for its whole life, and able to repair itself. That is the road to general intelligence, and it is the same mathematics that produces the universe.

Why transformers are a dead end

Scaling a frozen network does not make a mind.

A transformer is trained in a regime no animal is ever in: a static corpus in shuffled batches, a global gradient sent backwards through a stored computation graph, then a freeze. After that it never learns again. Every conversation starts from zero, its only memory is a window of recent tokens, and every fix is another round of training on more data with more compute. The industry's answer is to scale that up. Each tenfold increase in compute buys a few points on a benchmark, the training data of the internet is spent, and the one thing that would make the system intelligent, learning from its own life, is absent by construction. You cannot scale your way out of an architecture that has no way to remember what happened to it this morning.

  • Train, freeze, deployLearning stops the day the model ships. Everything it meets in production is forgotten when the context window scrolls. No brain works that way.
  • A global gradient through a stored graphBackpropagation needs every layer's activity kept for the backward pass and a global error routed to every weight. No neuron does that, and it is why training costs what it does.
  • No stream of thoughtThe model runs in discrete invocations with no persistent state. It does not think between calls, and it has no body and no ongoing life to learn from.
  • Replay instead of memoryLearning anything new means shuffled batches and replay buffers, because learning in the order life arrives makes it forget. Memory is bolted on outside the network.
  • Imagined and witnessed are the same thingA transformer has no distinction between what it generated and what it observed. That is where hallucination comes from, and no amount of scale removes it.
  • Logarithmic returns on exponential costTen times the compute for a few benchmark points. Ten times the data, which does not exist. The scaling curve is a bill, and it is the wrong curve.
How Cadence solves it

One brain. One mode. One life.

A Cadence brain is made of cortices that settle together into an equilibrium, where a transformer stacks feedforward layers. Each synapse changes from the activity of its own two neurons. Memory is written into the records the current reading touches, one witnessed outcome at a time. The same brain acts and learns from the moment it starts until it stops.

The design comes straight from our physics. An observer patch in Observer Patch Holography keeps records, reads its neighbours and repairs disagreement until the net settles. A Cadence cortex is that patch in software, and a thought is the state the brain settles into.

  • Learning never stopsThere is one mode. Each step takes in an observation and the outcome of the last action, updates memories and synapses, and issues the next action. No switch between learning and use, no deployment copy.
  • Local learning, no backward passEvery synapse reads the activity of its own two neurons and one broadcast error. No computation graph is stored, so a brain the size of a connectome learns on ordinary hardware.
  • A continuous stream of thoughtThe brain is one ongoing loop. Observations and rewards arrive while it is thinking, settled regions carry context across a delay, and thoughts linger.
  • Memory from one passA sparse reading touches few records, and the witnessed outcome is written into exactly those. One stream in the order it arrives, no replay ring, no shuffled batches.
  • Imagined futures stay imaginedThe brain settles each imagined future in isolation, compares the recorded consequences and acts on the best one. An imagined outcome never becomes a witnessed fact.
  • Built like biologyNeurons, synapses, cortices, a critic and an associative reward memory are the building blocks. A real connectome loads as plain data.
Embodied AGI

A robot that learns its job on the job.

The datacenter model of AI puts a frozen network behind an API and streams the robot's senses to it. A Cadence brain sits inside the machine. It learns the body it has from what its motors do, keeps learning as the body wears and the task changes, and decides in tens of milliseconds on a CPU. No round trip, no retraining run, no fleet-wide update to teach one robot one new thing.

Small local brain

Runs in a browser tab

Every brain on this page runs its whole life in JavaScript on your machine, decision for decision equal to the Python agent. Decision latency p95 is 52 ms for Connect Four and 75 ms for the artist. A robot controller needs no datacenter.

Learns the body it has

Motor babbling to competence

The arm learns its own kinematics from random movement and reaches 99% of held-out targets from one pass over its experience. Nobody wrote the body into it.

Adapts without a reset

Wear, damage, new tools

Lengthen the arm's upper link mid-life and it recovers to 99% with no reset, then returns to 99% on the original body. The artist keeps drawing when its body and its canvas change mid-drawing.

Safe imagination

Plans in its head, acts once

Before it moves, the brain imagines candidate actions from its records and picks the best. Imagined outcomes never become witnessed facts, so a plan that fails in the head costs nothing in the world.

From four lives in a tab to a fleet of bodies.

The same loop that runs the arm runs a warehouse picker, an inspection drone or a surgical assistant: observe, remember, predict, act, learn from what followed. Each machine learns its own job, and each keeps its brain.

Live in the browser

Four brains you can open and watch.

Each one runs the loop above and keeps learning while you interact with it. The whole brain is rendered live beside the task, neuron by neuron. Each ships its acceptance receipt and the verifier that recomputes the receipt from the event logs.

01 · Arm

A body learned from babbling.

A two-link arm moves at random and learns its own body from what follows each motor command. To copy what you draw, it searches over the consequences it has recorded and takes the command whose predicted displacement follows the line.

  • The forward model is a records cortex, written one witnessed transition at a time.
  • A longer upper link changes the body. The arm adapts with no reset, then returns to its original body.
  • The page continues one life in the browser with the whole brain live beside the arm.

02 · World

A world that must be remembered.

A 6 by 6 world of walls, doors, objects, colours and words, seen one cell at a time. One life learns what follows its actions, remembers where it saw an object, corrects that memory when the object moves, holds a cue across a delay and grounds words in objects.

  • A request names an object and begins in a distant cell under a deadline, so a remembered place is what makes it reachable in time.
  • Four stores keep witnessed evidence: object to place, cell to its passages, the last word seen, and word to referent.

03 · Connect Four

The rules, learned by watching.

One life learns what a dropped stone does and which windows of four cells are completed lines from the games it plays, and chooses its moves by searching over the boards it imagines from those records. The loop

Observe, remember, predict, act, learn.

Every Cadence brain lives in one mode. Each step takes in the current observation and the outcome of the previous action, updates its memories and its synapses, and issues the next action. No switch separates learning from use, and no point arrives at which learning has to stop.

  1. 01ObserveA reading arrives: the sensor fields, a flag for each field the senses left out, the action just taken and the reads of the brain's stores.
  2. 02RememberThe reading selects the cells that will hold its outcome, and the stores keep the witnessed evidence the application declares.
  3. 03PredictAn imagined consequence is a read of the records, so a supplied search can compare many candidate actions at the cost of a vector product each.
  4. 04ActThe brain issues one action and holds it pending. A clock tick alone is not feedback, so the pending action waits for its real outcome.
  5. 05Learn from the outcomeThe witnessed outcome is written into exactly the records that reading touched, and the settled regions change their synapses from their own neurons' activity and one broadcast error.
Live settlementNeurons relax to rest. Every few seconds a new input arrives and they settle again. The rest state is the thought.
Δwij = η · (s+i s+j − s−i s−j) / 2βThe free and nudged rule that moves the synapses of the settled regions. A connection reads the activity of its own two neurons, and no backward computation graph is stored through the brain.

Cadence builds on equilibrium propagation, on the sparse expansion with a local delta-rule readout that Marr and Albus described for the cerebellum, and on the observer consensus mathematics of our physics program. Each cortex declares its ports, every update reads a neighbourhood, and a real connectome loads as plain data.

Evidence

Every brain ships its receipt.

A run binds its outcomes to the code and the logs that produced them. The claim and the means to check it travel together.

01

Predicates

The receipt lists each acceptance predicate with its value, its threshold, how it was aggregated over seeds and whether it passed.

02

Event logs

Every decision and outcome of the life is written to an event log. The receipt carries the sha256 of each log, the library's commit and the hashes of the stage sources.

03

An independent verifier

A separate program reads the event logs and recomputes the receipt from them. Running it is how a reader checks the run without taking our word for it.

Get started

Install it in one line.

One line takes the library from its repository. NumPy is the only required dependency, and optional backends add Numba for fused CPU kernels, PyTorch for CUDA, and MLX for Apple silicon. Python 3.11 or newer, MIT licensed, fully typed.

GitHub → PyPI Quickstart Docs 
# install the library
$ pip install git+https://github.com/muellerberndt/cadence

import cadence as cd

brain = cd.GenericBrain.build(2, 3, hidden=16, seed=0)
action = brain.step([[position, goal]])   # no outcome yet

while alive:
    position, reward = world.apply(action)
    action = brain.step([[position, goal]], reward=[reward])

# observe, remember, predict, act, learn from the outcome
