Cadence · Experimental neural systems

Toward general intelligence through local repair.

Cadence researches a common architecture for learning, memory, planning and creativity: patch overlap consensus, equilibrium detuning and evolving self-reflection. A continuing network interprets observations, explores possible responses and repairs its organization from actual experience.

Illustration · interacting local states

The hypothesis

Fewer mechanisms should support more of what a learner needs: remembering, revising, predicting and exploring. The ambition is an animal-inspired learning system. Biological equivalence and general lifelong memory require evidence beyond the implemented interface.

The common core

Local state. Learned relationships.

Cadence connects bounded observer-like patches with local state, declared ports, readback and feedback. Overlap repair settles their relationships under observations. Equilibrium detuning changes learned connections; actual consequences provide evidence for the next repair.

TemporalPatchNet learns observed paths, carries context and privately repairs continuous action proposals under the same model. TemporalMemory protects selected local responses during further learning. Games, language, perception, embodiment and creative work are applications of these shared operations; broad transfer remains a research goal.

The interaction contract → Documentation
  • Repair under a shared boundaryAdjacent temporal patches share context while actual observations hold the boundary. Targets enter detuned teaching phases; imagined futures supply no observations.
  • Learn from local contrastsConnections change from local positive and negative detuning contrasts. Required phases must satisfy their numerical checks before an update commits. Teaching precision is supplied explicitly.
  • Retain different kinds of stateActivity carries short-term context. Learned connections survive activity reset. Selected response constraints can preserve an acquired path while other directions learn; capacity and importance selection are explicit limits.
  • Keep predictions separate from evidencePrivate continuations and action plans leave live state unchanged. The fixed-model actor admits actual readings once, and a changed goal cannot rewrite its factual past.

What the tests establish

Memory is a behavior to measure.

The paper measures retained behavior during further learning, useful action under disturbance, the information a compressed boundary preserves, acquisition in two phases on a sentence grammar, and the first two rungs of the metacognition ladder, each accepted by a demo that works with the rung on and fails with it off against a hand-designed control. Each result includes its controls and assumptions.

01 · Memory

Retain one response while learning another

In a bounded feature-prediction test, all three temporal models preserve one selected descriptor sequence while learning another. Local response constraints and supplied weighting support this result. Acquisition, retention and musical quality still need joint tests on performed sound; available rank alone does not guarantee usable learning capacity.

02 · Action

Repair a plan from actual consequences

A body relation learned from position and action experience supports joint future-state and action repair. In a small control task, feedback improves recovery from disturbance over myopic control and an initial open-loop plan. The goal and physical coordinates are supplied.

03 · Compression

A smaller boundary can preserve a past

Under fixed linear-Gaussian assumptions, one retained observation and a compressed prefix reproduce the estimates and actions of a longer window. The measured persistent payload falls from 272 to 208 bytes. Changing the model invalidates that summary.

04 · Attention

A brain that reads its own surprise

A governor patch that reads the brain's own surprise catches 0.961 of the laser dots awake 0.238 of the time at 15.3 patch moments per decision, where a brain that is always awake catches 0.962 at 71.3 and one that never wakes 0.249. A steering patch that sets the gain of each sense inside the repair weighs the eye at 1.8 when the voice is displaced and the ear at 1.8 in the dark, with an error of 0.041 where fixed gains give 0.081 and the designed rule 0.062. In all four acceptance demos selection over the genome beat the hand-set genome and a random search. The cat runs in your browser as the fifth example, and a fruit fly's whole nervous system as the sixth: A fly in the Matrix.

Read the results with their assumptions.

The paper binds each result to its implementation, controls and evidence. These bounded tests support reusable learning, memory and action mechanisms. General intelligence, useful recursive self-reflection and creativity require further evidence across applications; recall or interpolation alone does not establish original creation.

Watch a brain settle, learn and act.

Three quickstart brains run behind a local page on your machine: one install, one command each. Nothing is hosted and nothing is trained ahead of time; the brain trains in front of you in seconds, from fixed seeds, so every run is the same. The page draws every neuron and synapse, colours the neurons by their distance from equilibrium, shows the synapses' last change from the detuning contrast, plots the learning as it is measured, and says in words what the brain is doing.

cadence-demo decide

A settling brain decides

The task. Five senses, one at a time, and three possible actions; sense k asks for action k mod 3. The brain answers with its most active motor neuron, is then shown the action that was wanted, and has to learn the right action for all five senses.

The equilibrium. The brain settles until no neuron moves; that state is its answer. To learn, it settles once more with the chosen action nudged, and every synapse moves on the difference of the two settled states. Nothing is propagated backward.

cadence-demo stream

A record patch remembers in one shot and sleeps

The task. Three streams of eight symbols, numbers from 0 to 11, one moment at a time. At every moment the brain has to say one of five outcomes: the current symbol plus the previous one, modulo 5. It is never told the rule, only shown the right outcome after each moment.

The equilibrium. The settled context path reads each moment; by day the outcome is written once into sixteen of 2,048 record cells and recalled after a single pass. By night, with the streams closed, the slow weights learn the store's own completions. At dawn the brain says every outcome right with its memory erased.

A fly in the Matrix

A whole nervous system flies a body and learns

The brain. The 150,802 neurons of an adult fruit fly, brain and nerve cord wired as measured (BANC release 888, 1.86 million synapse classes), as one settling brain at one global gain. Two physiology gates hold it to the literature against shuffled wirings; the wingbeat reflex, which rests on spike timing, is supplied and declared.

The life. A rigid body with stroke-averaged aerodynamics in a green wireframe room, the whole nervous system drawn where it sits with the settling live, the fly in close-up, its compound eyes' view beside it. It lives at the animal's rates and learns which smell means sugar at its mushroom body's own synapses, from the sugar you place and the blows you deal, with the same rule that runs the receipted T-maze experiment. cadence-demo body

A temporal patch learns a consequence and plans

The task. A body on a line: one measured position, one push per step, a rule the brain is never shown. First learn from random pushes how the body moves, well enough to predict six steps ahead on unseen paths. Then drive the body to the goal and hold it there through an unannounced shove, planning six pushes ahead under the learned model and replanning from what is measured.

The equilibrium. The free path is the zero-defect solution of the patch's energy. The observed positions detune it by plus and minus beta and the synapses move on the difference; the same contrast on the push port, with the synapses frozen, is the plan.

Get started

Observe, repair, inspect.

Cadence 0.14.0 uses Python 3.11 or newer and NumPy for temporal learning, one-shot records that sleep, a belief patch that imagines under action, and planning. The supported graph APIs also offer optional accelerator backends. The library is MIT licensed.

This small example exercises the current temporal interface. The full interaction guide learns an action/consequence relation and repairs actions after actual readback. A successful call alone does not establish task competence.

Quickstarts →Build your own brainFull guidePyPI
import numpy as np
import cadence as cd

net = cd.TemporalPatchNet(
    inputs=2, hidden=8, outputs=1, seed=7
)
# One observed path: batch, time, ports.
inputs = np.array([[[0.2, 0.8]]])
observed = np.array([[[0.6]]])
result = net.observe(inputs, observed)
print(result.reason, result.free.output)

# Reset activity; preserve learned relationships.
net.reset()
