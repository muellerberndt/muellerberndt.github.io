The Cadence brain

A deep real-time brain that learns from experience.

Small predictive patches work together to find an answer. System 1 is the default brain that learns, remembers and acts. System 2 adds optional observers that feed back into the same brain.

Why Cadence / Embodied AI

Built like a brain.
Made for a body.

Animal and human brains learn from experience and not by backpropagation with gradient descent. Cadence is designed the same way: its connections change locally while the brain keeps running.

On the job

It learns while it works.

The same brain acts and learns from every measured outcome. There is no training phase before deployment and no difference between training and inference.

Changed conditions

It adapts when the body changes.

A worn actuator, a new payload or a different surface shows up in what the robot measures. The live brain adjusts from those measurements, without being told what changed.

Local learning

It learns the way brains do.

Each connection changes from the activity of the two units it joins. There is no backward pass through the network, so learning can run on the robot itself.

Memory

It remembers.

A working trace carries the recent past and an associative memory keeps what mattered. One experience is recorded, and the next decision in that situation reads it.

Settled answers

It acts only on a settled answer.

Every action is the settled state of the whole brain. A brain that does not settle refuses to act, so a controller can fall back instead of executing a guess.

Inspection

It shows its work.

The activity of every region can be read while the brain runs. Private imagination tests a response to a situation before the body commits to it.

Shown in simulation today in Rover Lab, Patch World and the Atari Arcade. Cadence 0.70 is experimental research software, and a physical robot evaluation is the next test.

How this differs from backpropagation

A conventional network is trained by a backward pass that carries errors through every layer, usually offline over a dataset, and is then frozen for use. In Cadence's default brain each synapse compares the activity of its two neurons in two settled states, one free and one nudged by the outcome, and changes by their difference. Reward scales that change, and memory writes are local too. The brain keeps no backward computation graph.

Under idealized conditions this local contrast follows the gradient that backpropagation would compute. Networks trained by backpropagation can also be recurrent, online and memory-using; the comparison is of update mechanisms. What the brain acquires is measured through its free behavior.

System 1 by default.
System 2 when it earns its cost.

Each bounded, self-reading patch has local state, incoming ports, a prediction and records of experience. Patches repair their disagreement together until the whole brain settles on one answer.

Observing regions read the rest of the brain and return their influence inside the same settlement. They add recursive feedback to System 1 without replacing it. Whether they help is measured for each task.

The mechanism

Every answer is the settled state of the whole brain. A layout whose patches never settle against one another does not build, and a brain that does not settle refuses to act instead of guessing.

System 1 and System 2 name functional roles in software. They are not two biological brains or a ranking of intelligence. The design is experimental: observers are available in every brain, and their benefit is measured task by task.

Advanced engines, capability and cost

Cadence 0.70.0 keeps specialized engines beside the main brain, each with its own contract. In the population solver, populations of patches settle one joint energy and observers read exact prediction errors; the interactive model below shows its equations. Record patches hold event records and consolidate them. A temporal model learns the consequences of actions and plans with them. What changes during settlement and learning?

For a query, weights and biases stay fixed while internal states settle. When Cadence admits an experience, supplied outcomes can constrain outputs and the relation parameters can change too, anchored to their values before that experience. Both layouts use that learning API; this illustration keeps parameters fixed.

State connections let a later constraint revise an earlier state. Observers also bring exact prediction errors into that interaction. Returning arrows show influence through the objective's derivatives, not additional learned reverse edges. Changing the sensory signal retains activity; changing the layout resets it.

Observer depth, repair steps and prediction into the future are three different things.

Live operation guide ↗
The patch equations behind the illustration

predictionᵢ = tanh(biasᵢ + Σ weightᵢⱼ × signalⱼ)
errorᵢ = stateᵢ − predictionᵢ
objective = ½ Σ errorᵢ² + 0.18/2 × Σ stateᵢ²

A signal can be a supplied input, another patch's state or an observed prediction error. Predictions and errors are recomputed from the current state; errors cannot be adjusted independently to conceal disagreement.

The illustration uses bounded state updates, exact derivatives through the error readbacks and backtracking for objective decrease. It stops at its stationarity tolerance or a 240-step budget. The objective's numerical “energy” does not measure electrical energy.

The positive activity penalty matters. For the corresponding acyclic, unclamped model in the idealized zero-penalty limit, the stationary answer reduces to forward substitution. Positive penalties or output constraints allow a different compromise. That difference alone does not establish better task performance.
