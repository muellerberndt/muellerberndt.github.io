Observer Patch Holography

The Theory of Everything.

Observer Patch Holography asks whether the major parts of physics can begin with one simple arrangement: local systems keep records, compare what they share, and repair disagreements. It is a research program about physics unification, from quantum theory and gravity to particle physics.

OPH begins with no fitted numerical inputs. It has mathematical work about local records and repair, and it studies possible routes into familiar physics. A local closure calculation has a verified mathematical solution. Its link to a measured physical quantity is open. A separate proposal about the universe's record capacity has no selected physical cosmological value and makes no prediction.

For guided study, start with OPH Textbooks. For the formal route, use the papers and the lab; for the unification-first framing, see the physics unification page. For the simulation-first framing, see the simulation theory page.

Why OPH reaches for a wider picture

A theory of everything tries to explain why the successful parts of physics fit together. That includes quantum theory, gravity, particles, spacetime, and the observers who measure them.

OPH starts with limited local viewpoints. A fact becomes public only when different viewpoints can compare their records and settle their disagreement. The project asks whether familiar physical structure can grow from that rule.

The work separates formal results from physical interpretation. Some finite models and mathematical relations can be checked directly. Links to observed matter, cosmological values, and a full theory of nature remain open research work.

OPH also explores a self-reference question: can a universe and the record of itself made from within be two views of one system? The physical bridge needed to answer that question is open.

This connects the project to simulation theory. The proposal concerns information, records, and stable agreement in a physical world. The microphysics paper describes the detailed hardware model.

What the project investigates

  • How quantum records can arise from local agreement.
  • How a shared-record framework might lead to relativistic geometry and gravity under stated conditions.
  • How a twelve-port model can produce a mathematical symmetry pattern related to the Standard Model.
  • How string theory might connect to the framework's edge structure.
  • How a physical world could support a stable, self-reading information process.
  • Which additional physical arguments are needed before these mathematical routes describe nature.

FAQ

Short answers.

Does OPH replace established physics?

OPH explores a possible deeper account of familiar physics. Established theories remain the descriptions used for their tested domains.

Is the scope really that broad?

Yes. OPH asks about the laws of physics, the emergence of observers, and the possibility of a self-consistent universe. Its results cover different parts of that question at different levels of completion.

Does this overlap with simulation theory?

Yes. OPH develops simulation theory as a proposal about local records, repair, and a stable shared world. The detailed model is in the microphysics paper.

What does physics unification mean here?

It means OPH studies quantum theory, gravity, and particle physics from one starting point: local systems that have to agree on shared records. The routes have different results and open steps.

What is the fastest way to inspect the claim?

Read the main paper, then the microphysics paper, then move into the lab and the SM/GR derivation paper.
