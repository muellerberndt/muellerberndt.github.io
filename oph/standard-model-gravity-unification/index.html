What OPH unifies

OPH uses agreement between local viewpoints as the common mechanism. Each observer has limited access, and neighboring observers must be able to reconcile what they share. The project asks whether a stable public world can grow from that requirement.

The gravity route studies how this agreement may lead to relativistic geometry. The particle route studies a twelve-port model and a related symmetry pattern. Both routes have formal results and additional physical links that remain open.

A different kind of unification question

Many unification proposals start with a larger particle symmetry. OPH starts with local systems that must agree on shared records. It then asks whether that condition can constrain both gravity and particle physics.

The phrase Standard Model unified with gravity refers to the shared observer architecture behind both routes. The target is one framework for spacetime, gravity, particle structure, records, and observers.

FAQ

Short answers.

Does OPH unify the Standard Model with gravity?

OPH studies gravity and particle physics from one shared-record framework. The gravity route has stated conditions. A specified twelve-port model gives mathematical structure related to the Standard Model, while its connection to physical matter and a complete particle theory remains open.

What does Standard Model unified with gravity mean here?

It means OPH uses the same starting point for two separate research routes: gravity and particle physics. The aim is to see whether both can describe one physical world, while keeping the unresolved links clear.

Where is the formal argument?

The spacetime and Einstein paper covers the gravity route. The Standard Model gauge paper covers the two particle-physics routes. The synthesis paper connects their place in the wider project.
