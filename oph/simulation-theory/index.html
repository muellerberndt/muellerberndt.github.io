Simulation Theory in Physics.

OPH treats simulation theory as a physics question: could a world be built from local systems that keep records, compare what they share, repair disagreements, and settle into a shared history? It begins with no fitted numerical inputs.

OPH gives the idea of an informational world a concrete research program. The simulator-hardware side appears in Federated Echosahedral Screen Microphysics, which describes patches, records, synchronization, and public evidence.

The project also studies whether this starting point can connect to fundamental physics, including quantum theory, gravity, and particle physics. Each route has its own evidence, conditions, and required physical inputs.

A local mathematical closure calculation has a verified solution. No link from that solution to a measured physical quantity is derived. A separate proposal asks whether the capacity of shared records could relate to the cosmological constant. That proposal has no selected physical value and makes no prediction.

For the wider picture, visit the physics unification page, the theory of everything page, or the OPH hub.

Why OPH matters for simulation theory

Simulation theory often begins with a broad question about whether reality has an informational basis. OPH tries to make that question testable by defining local systems, shared records, and rules for resolving disagreement.

The goal is a physics program with clear mathematical and physical checks. The microphysics paper develops a hardware model, and the other papers test routes into gravity, quantum theory, and particle physics.

What OPH adds

  • Local systems with limited viewpoints.

  • A self-reference question about a world that can read itself from within.
FAQ

Short answers for simulation theory readers.

Is OPH saying the universe is literally a simulation?

OPH describes reality as a closed system that can form a stable shared account of itself. No physical bridge needed to prove a unique self-consistent result is derived. The proposal does not depend on an external programmer or a video-game analogy.

Why connect simulation theory to a theory of everything?

Because OPH asks whether the same record-and-repair framework can reach quantum theory, gravity, and particle physics. The papers state the evidence and limits for each route.

What does physics unification mean here?

It means OPH studies several parts of physics from one starting point: local systems that must agree on shared records. The routes have different levels of support and require different physical inputs.

Where should a skeptical reader start?

Start with the OPH hub, then read the theory-of-everything page, the book, and the main paper in that order.
