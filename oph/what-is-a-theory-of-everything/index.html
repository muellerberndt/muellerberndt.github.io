Plain Definition

What is a theory of everything?

A theory of everything tries to explain why the successful pieces of physics fit together: quantum mechanics, spacetime, gravity, particles, constants, and observers.

OPH approaches that problem with one mechanism. Finite local systems compare what they share, repair disagreements, and form stable public records.

OPH has a local mathematical closure calculation with a verified solution. Its link to a measured physical quantity is open. It also explores whether the capacity of shared records could relate to the cosmological constant. That proposal is incomplete, has no selected physical value, and makes no prediction.

What the phrase should mean

A serious theory of everything has to connect pieces that are usually studied separately. It has to say how quantum theory, spacetime, gravity, particle physics, and observation belong to one world.

OPH explores whether they can be different views of one shared-record architecture. The same language of local systems, comparison, repair, and records is used across the framework.

What OPH claims

Observer Patch Holography explores the idea that reality is a stable public world built from finite local viewpoints agreeing on shared data. The formal papers develop separate routes into quantum theory, relativity, particle structure, screen microphysics, and self-reference.

The larger claim is that one rule about shared records may organize the physics stack. The papers keep the mathematical results and the open physical links separate.
