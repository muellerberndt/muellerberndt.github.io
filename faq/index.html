Inside it, the world evolves through local disagreement and repair: neighbors compare records, repair what disagrees, and what survives every comparison is fact. Its observers reverse-engineer the rules behind their records and build the process that realizes this same history. Reaching that event is a mathematically guaranteed fixed point, and it exists in our subjective future just as our past exists. Nothing outside the history is needed to start it.

#What does it mean to call the universe a fixed-point solver?

A fixed point is an object returned unchanged by a specified map: applying F to x gives x again. A solver searches for an object satisfying that condition. OPH's closure proposal asks for a history whose internally recovered description, when realized, reproduces the same observations and interventions. A Sudoku is an accessible analogy: the finished grid simultaneously satisfies the row, column, box and clue constraints. A solver's sequence of guesses is different from the grid's defining relations. Likewise, no external clock has to run a universe-wide search before time begins. Different maps can have no fixed point, one or many. Writing the equation supplies a constraint; constructing the inhabited physical solution supplies the substantive work.

#How can two different descriptions of nature both be right?

Two descriptions are both right when a permitted translation turns one into the other everywhere both describers looked. #How does the universe stay consistent everywhere if each part only checks its neighbors?

Each part fixes disagreements with its neighbors, and the result is consistent everywhere exactly when the records around every closed loop cancel, which no single pair can check. Three people in separate rooms each hold a coin. Each pair meets and records only same or different, and all three pairs record different. Any two records fit together. All three cannot, because counting different as 1, with one plus one making zero, the loop totals 1 where it needs 0. Flip one record and repair converges anyway, leaving exactly one unit of disagreement that can be moved and never removed. That stubborn leftover is what a particle turns out to be.

#Can local repair find a self-consistent universe in polynomial time?

We conjecture that local repair lets the universe find a consistent solution to its own creation history in polynomial time. A history closes on itself when its observers recover the law from their records, build a system that runs it, and reproduce what they saw, including how their experiments turned out. How could they search without seeing the whole universe? Two neighbors check a record they share. A failed check tells them where to repair it. The next neighbor sees the change and can check again. The same operation drives the search and verifies the result. For a network evaluating a Boolean formula on a given input, we can count the work. Visit its n checks in dependency order and one pass gives the correct answer. This bound needs no entanglement. For a whole creation history, we still need to show that the work grows no faster than a fixed power of the network size.

What counts as a repair step?

  1. Hold a proposed record fixed and check each overlap once. If every check passes, that record is a certificate of consensus. During a search, a failed check also identifies a place to rewrite. The time to reach consensus depends on how often checks have to be revisited.
  2. The classical seam rule gives a small example. If two ports hold 2 and 0, balancing leaves them with 1 and 1. Their total is conserved and the sum of their squares falls from 4 to 2. Each strict balancing move lowers the sum of squares by at least 2. For N ports with integer loads from 0 to a fixed M, there are at most NM²/2 such moves. This count covers strict balancing of classical loads. Idle checks and coherent quantum evolution have their own accounting.
  3. For a compiled Boolean formula on a given input, every computed register feeds at most one later check. Any update order then makes at most n(n+1)/2 repairs that actually change the record. The dependency-order sweep reaches consensus in at most n attempts, including checks that already pass.
  4. The dependence on the wiring and the schedule can be tested. Allow a register to feed several later checks and an explicit n-node network admits an update order with 2ⁿ − 1 genuine repairs. Even on the compiled formulas, a scheduler that eventually visits a failing check may spend arbitrarily long checking other sites first.

For the full closure claim, one would have to specify a growing family of physical histories, count the resources used to recover and realize each description, and show that the reconstructed records and responses agree. An efficient construction would establish possibility. Proving it was the only efficient construction would also require ruling out alternatives.

#Can reality contradict itself?

No, because a contradiction asserts every world at once, and a description that permits everything rules nothing out. Picture a bank ledger saying an account holds nothing and also a hundred pounds. #What is quantum entanglement?

An OPH cell is an observer with 12 ports at the corners of an icosahedron. A connection between two ports is called a seam. Put one unit of load at the left port and let the two ports exchange it. #Why do we have a sense of music?

We have a sense of music because hearing runs the same computation that runs the Universe, and music is sound built to exercise it. the math behind this answer

From a symmetric repair to octaves, twelve notes and release

  1. Symmetry decides what a repair can settle into. A rule that treats all 60 rotations of a cell alike can only settle its twelve readings into patterns that the rotations carry into one another: one total, three directions, five and three more. Each fades at its own fixed rate, 0.9539, 0.9 and 0.8794 per pass, and the slowest readable one is space. Group theory calls this Schur's lemma, and it holds for anything that settles under a symmetry. OPH's law is one fixed rule, the same at every step, so the physics built on it treats every moment alike, and an oscillator that comes back to its own state comes back again and again. A sound that repeats every period T is unchanged by a shift of T, and the only patterns such a shift leaves alone are pure tones at one, two, three and more times 1/T. That list is the harmonic series. It plays the part for a repeating sound that the four bands play for the cell.
  2. Hearing is agreement on a period. The cochlea splits sound into thousands of frequency channels, and the brain has to decide which of them came from one source. Channels whose frequencies share a period are heard as one sound with one pitch. Play 200, 300 and 400 hertz together and you hear 100 hertz, the period all three agree on, although no channel carries it. Telephone voices rely on this, since the line drops the lowest frequencies. Mistune one overtone by a few percent and it splits off as a separate tone. Pitch is a fact the channels hold in common, made by agreement, the way OPH makes physical facts.
  3. The octave sounds the same and different for an exact reason. A tone at twice the frequency repeats twice in each period of the lower tone, so it also repeats once in each. Every overtone of the upper tone is an overtone of the lower one, and the two together still repeat with the lower tone's period. A detector listening for the lower tone's period finds it in the upper tone too, which carries a shorter period on top. The shared period makes it the same note and the shorter one makes it higher, so pitch is a helix that goes once around the circle of note names per octave while it climbs. The fusion is universal: Tsimane' listeners in the Bolivian Amazon, who grew up with little Western music, take two notes in a simple ratio for a single sound more often than other pairs, as Westerners do. Treating notes an octave apart as one note is learned. Asked to sing tones back, US listeners often land a whole number of octaves away on the same note name, while the Tsimane' keep the intervals and ignore the note name. An observer settles on the agreements its surroundings offer.
  4. Twelve comes from the arithmetic of 2 and 3. After 2 the next new overtone is 3, and with octaves set aside it gives the fifth, 3 to 2, the next simplest agreement. A stack of fifths never lands exactly on a stack of octaves, because a power of 3 is odd and a power of 2 is even. The best near misses come from the continued fraction of the base-two logarithm of 3/2 and close after 5, 12, 41 and 53 fifths. Twelve is the shortest cycle that comes back within a quarter of a semitone: twelve fifths overshoot seven octaves by 1.36 percent, the Pythagorean comma. In that cycle the fifths miss 3 to 2 by a fiftieth of a semitone and the major thirds miss 5 to 4 by about a seventh of one. Five-note scales and the seven-note major scale are runs of consecutive fifths, and the twelve-note cycle holds them in every key. Cycles of 41 and 53 close better, and 53 appears in Turkish music theory, while scales around the world keep to seven or fewer notes per octave.
  5. The twelve notes and the twelve ports are different twelves. The ports come from counting the corners of a closed surface, the notes from the arithmetic of 2 and 3, and what the two share is the operation that tunes them. The comma is a loop defect: go once around the circle of fifths and you miss your starting point. However twelve fifths are tuned so that the loop closes on seven octaves, their departures from a pure fifth add up to the same 23.46 cents, so tuning can move the defect and never remove it. Equal temperament spreads it evenly, about two cents per fifth, and every key works. Meantone tuning narrowed eleven fifths further to make the major thirds pure and piled the remainder onto one unusable fifth, the wolf. This is repair in miniature. A disagreement that cannot vanish is spread smoothly or gathered in one place, and gathered in one place it is a knot, the way OPH describes a particle.
  6. Two tones in a ratio p to q, in lowest terms, sound together as one pattern that repeats every q periods of the lower tone: once for the octave, twice for the fifth, three times for the fourth, four times for the major third and fifteen times for the semitone, 16 to 15. The simpler the ratio, the sooner the channels agree and the more overtones the tones share. Across more than 250 listeners, liking for consonant chords tracks liking for harmonic overtones. Overtones that nearly match throb in loudness, a beat as many times per second as their frequencies differ, and the throbbing stops at exact agreement. Musicians tune by ear until it stops, which is repair by listening. Tension is a disagreement held open and resolution is its repair. Listeners release dopamine in one part of the striatum while anticipating a musical peak and in another at the peak itself. In OPH experience is repair owned by an observer and read from the inside, so a completed repair is a felt release. The Tsimane' dislike rough, beating sounds as Westerners do, yet rate consonant and dissonant chords as equally pleasant. The physics of agreement is the same for everyone, and which agreement feels like coming home is learned.
  7. Rhythm uses the same small whole numbers in time. A beat is a shared clock, and meter nests beats in twos and threes. Play three beats against two, at three and two beats per second, speed it up a hundredfold, and the clicks fuse into tones of 300 and 200 hertz, a fifth. Rhythm and harmony are one ratio heard at two speeds. OPH has no common clock. Clocks agree by comparing counts when observers meet. A room dancing to one beat is many clocks brought into agreement, and groove is that agreement kept alive by small timing errors that keep being repaired. People who have just sung or moved in synchrony cooperate more in group economic games afterwards.
