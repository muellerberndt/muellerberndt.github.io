### Why is there something rather than nothing?

There is something because a structure that accounts for itself, with no outside cause and nothing added, is the only answer left once space and time are off the table. "Something outside made it" needs an outside and a before, the very things in question. "It is just here" explains nothing and forbids nothing. Self-consistency remains, and it is a stronger demand than it sounds. Pick any positive number, divide one by it, add one, and repeat. Every start lands on 1.618, the golden ratio, because one demand that refers to itself picks a single number out of a continuum. Demand nothing and you get no form. Demand a little and you get very nearly one world. And since consistency belongs to descriptions, that world must contain something that keeps records and can be wrong.

### How can two different descriptions of nature both be right?

Two descriptions are both right when a permitted translation turns one into the other everywhere both describers looked. On opposite sides of a Barcelona street, a delivery van hides a food truck from Bob, so he reports none and Alice reports one. Both are accurate, because only what both saw can be held against them. On that shared part, Alice has the van on her left and Bob on his right, and swapping left and right turns her account into his, the way a clock treats 3 and 15 as the same hour. If she puts the van two car-lengths from the corner and he puts it four, no relabeling explains the gap, and a record has to change. Translations around a closed loop must also return to the start, or a round trip would make something from nothing.

### How does the universe stay consistent everywhere if each part only checks its neighbors?

Each part fixes disagreements with its neighbors, and the result is consistent everywhere exactly when the records around every closed loop cancel, which no single pair can check. Three people in separate rooms each hold a coin. Each pair meets and records only same or different, and all three pairs record different. Any two records fit together. All three cannot, because counting different as 1, with one plus one making zero, the loop totals 1 where it needs 0. Wire twelve observers as the corners of a twenty-sided solid and repair brings all 2,048 starting states to the one consistent state. Flip one record and repair converges anyway, leaving exactly one unit of disagreement that can be moved and never removed. That stubborn leftover is what a particle turns out to be.

### Why can nothing go faster than light?

Nothing outruns light because influence spreads by reading, and every read has a maximum reach. ## The quantum world

### Why is the world quantum?

The world is quantum because the questions an observer can put to it do not all commute, so asking one and then another differs from the reverse order. That refusal is what ties observers together. If every question could be answered alongside every other, each observer's answers would be an independent row in a giant spreadsheet, and nothing one observer held would constrain what another holds. Questions that commute with everything collect in one corner, called the center, and that is where records live. That corner looks exactly like classical physics, a world of tables and pointer readings. A record layer like that cannot change smoothly and reversibly, so whatever flows in the world flows in the part that refuses to commute. With the questions fixed, the rule for odds has one possible form, the Born rule.

### Why can't you know position and momentum at the same time?

You can't know both because position and momentum are two questions with no joint answer. Socks then shoes is a different operation from shoes then socks, and asking position then momentum likewise differs from asking momentum then position. The difference between the two orders, called the commutator, is a fixed amount with Planck's constant in it. No apparatus appears in that statement, so no better apparatus shrinks it. The uncertainty relation reads that amount as a floor under the spread in position times the spread in momentum. Squeeze an electron into an atom-sized region, a tenth of a nanometer across, and the spread in its speed cannot drop below about six hundred kilometers a second. There is no state of the electron with a sharp position and a sharp speed, so the measurement has nothing to disturb.

### What is quantum entanglement?

Entanglement is definiteness that lives in the relation between two systems and in neither one alone. A playing card shows the idea. “The seven of hearts” splits into a fact about the suit and a fact about the rank. “The red seven or the black eight” fixes how color and rank go together while leaving both unsettled, and learning one hands you the other. Most joint descriptions are of that second kind. Cut an entangled pair in two and each half on its own comes back vague, and that vagueness measures how much the cut severed. Quantum pairs go one step further. There is no hidden face underneath the relation for it to describe, which is why entangled pairs agree more often than any pre-arranged answer sheet allows. The relation is the object.

### Is entanglement "spooky action at a distance"?

No. Nothing travels between entangled particles, and nothing done on one side can send a message to the other. ### Why do quantum correlations beat classical physics, but only by 2√2?

Quantum correlations beat classical ones because each side's two questions refuse to be answered together, and they stop at 2√2 because that refusal has a maximum size. ### What happens when a quantum system is measured?

A measurement writes a record, and writing a record deletes every comparison between the possible answers. Picture a mail room. ### What is a particle?

A particle is a knot in the way neighboring patches of the world translate each other's readings, a twist that no local repair can undo. ### What is the wavefunction?

The wavefunction is a way of writing down what an observer can ask and the odds of each answer. Different observers with different lists of questions build different spaces, and they agree wherever their lists overlap. That overlap is the only sense in which the wavefunction is public. ### What is the Born rule?

The Born rule says the probability of a quantum outcome is the square of its amplitude, and it is the only odds rule that gives a question the same weight however it is asked. ## Energy, heat and information

### What is energy?

Energy is the rate at which a region's records are worked through by repair, counted on that region's own clock. The world has one move. Two neighbors share a seam, their readings disagree, and repair brings both to the midpoint. How fast a region is being worked through is its energy, and in physics language it is what generates the region's advance in time, the way momentum generates sliding. The familiar properties follow from the move. Disagreement is a sum of squares with a floor at zero, so energy is never negative. Regions with no seam between them have nothing to repair across, so their energies add. Each repair takes from one reading exactly what it gives the other, like double-entry bookkeeping, so energy is conserved, and a perpetual motion machine asks for a move that does not exist.

### Why does E = mc²?

E = mc² holds because mass is energy that stays put, a standing cost of repair on the region's own clock. A region's energy is how fast its records are being worked through by repair. Mass is the cost of a pattern that keeps re-asserting itself, like the heap of water John Scott Russell chased along a canal, which held its shape while the water under it changed. Write a region's energy and its three components of momentum into a two-by-two table of numbers, and the table's determinant, a quantity every observer agrees on, is the square of the mass. Set the momentum to zero and energy equals mass. Restore the speed of light and E = mc². Because the conversion is squared, a helium nucleus's tiny mass shortfall against its four parts releases 28.3 million electronvolts.

### What is entropy?

Entropy is the average number of yes-or-no questions it would take to say exactly which arrangement something is in. A fair coin is one bit, one question. A fair eight-sided die is three. A coin weighted nine to one comes to under half a bit, since you can usually guess. Entropy is a property of a description, of somebody's odds over the possibilities, and different odds give a different number for the same object. The same quantity turns up on five instruments built by people who did not know they were measuring one thing. An engine reads it as heat divided by temperature, a gas as Boltzmann's count of arrangements, a memory as Shannon's count of questions, a black hole as the area of its horizon, and a public record as what a settled disagreement throws away.

### Why does writing information down cost heat?

Writing a record costs heat because settling a question throws away the alternatives, and what is thrown away has to leave as heat. Rolf Landauer showed in 1961 that looking is free and forgetting is not. Taking two distinguishable states of a memory down to one discards a distinction, and the only exit a memory has is heat, at least 2.87×10⁻²¹ joules per bit at room temperature, less in the cold. Writing a record is the same operation. Two readings that disagree hold two possibilities, and settling them leaves one. Copying onto blank memory postpones the bill, and clearing that memory later costs the same. In 2012 a bead in an optical trap showed the heat per erasure settling onto Landauer's floor. ### Why do living things die?

A living thing lasts for as long as it can pay for itself. A standing cost is a rate, a rate is energy, and the budget that pays it is finite. Walter Cannon, who named this holding homeostasis in 1926, had spent the First World War with wounded men dying of shock, and his list of the quantities a body holds steady was a list of the readings he had watched go. ### Does evolution have a goal?

No. Natural selection is three things running together, and none of them aims at anything. Something copies itself imperfectly, the imperfection passes to the copies, and some versions end up with more copies than others. There is no fourth item in which anybody decides. In Richard Lenski's laboratory, daily since 1988, a pipette has moved one hundredth of each of twelve flasks of E. coli into a fresh flask. It cannot tell one cell from another, yet the populations change in a consistent direction. Fitter is a bookkeeping word. A fitter lineage holds a larger share of tomorrow's flask than of today's, and the word predicts nothing about the day after, because the flask it competes in is the flask it is changing. Mean fitness, measured against thawed ancestors, climbed through fifty thousand generations with no sign of leveling off.
