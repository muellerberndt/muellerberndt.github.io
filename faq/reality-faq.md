## Time

### What is time?

Time is three different things sharing one word: order, direction and flow. Order is which event depended on which. A later repair certifies that it read a value an earlier one wrote, and pairs with no such chain between them have no fact about which came first. Direction comes from the repair itself, which can average seven possible pasts of a seam into one reading and destroy the difference. Flow is what a region's own state makes its questions do, a reversible motion computed from the odds it holds, with its rate built in. A clock joins them, as an observer that counts every repair touching it and turns the count into phase. Tallies, waves, light signals and a region's own flow all read one parameter, because each is a reading of the same repair history.

## The quantum world

### Why is the world quantum?

The world is quantum because the questions an observer can put to it do not all commute, so asking one and then another differs from the reverse order. That refusal is what ties observers together. If every question could be answered alongside every other, each observer's answers would be an independent row in a giant spreadsheet, and nothing one observer held would constrain what another holds. Questions that commute with everything collect in one corner, called the center, and that is where records live. That corner looks exactly like classical physics, a world of tables and pointer readings. A record layer like that cannot change smoothly and reversibly, so whatever flows in the world flows in the part that refuses to commute. With the questions fixed, the rule for odds has one possible form, the Born rule.

### Why can't you know position and momentum at the same time?

You can't know both because position and momentum are two questions with no joint answer. ## Energy, heat and information

### What is energy?

Energy is the rate at which a region's records are worked through by repair, counted on that region's own clock. The world has one move. Two neighbors share a seam, their readings disagree, and repair brings both to the midpoint. How fast a region is being worked through is its energy, and in physics language it is what generates the region's advance in time, the way momentum generates sliding. The familiar properties follow from the move. Disagreement is a sum of squares with a floor at zero, so energy is never negative. Regions with no seam between them have nothing to repair across, so their energies add. Each repair takes from one reading exactly what it gives the other, like double-entry bookkeeping, so energy is conserved, and a perpetual motion machine asks for a move that does not exist.

### Why does E = mc²?

E = mc² holds because mass is energy that stays put, a standing cost of repair on the region's own clock. A region's energy is how fast its records are being worked through by repair. Mass is the cost of a pattern that keeps re-asserting itself, like the heap of water John Scott Russell chased along a canal, which held its shape while the water under it changed. Write a region's energy and its three components of momentum into a two-by-two table of numbers, and the table's determinant, a quantity every observer agrees on, is the square of the mass. Set the momentum to zero and energy equals mass. Restore the speed of light and E = mc². Because the conversion is squared, a helium nucleus's tiny mass shortfall against its four parts releases 28.3 million electronvolts.

### What is entropy?

Entropy is the average number of yes-or-no questions it would take to say exactly which arrangement something is in. A fair coin is one bit, one question. A fair eight-sided die is three. ### Why does writing information down cost heat?

Writing a record costs heat because settling a question throws away the alternatives, and what is thrown away has to leave as heat. ### Why do Newton's laws work so well if they are not the final word?

Newton's laws work because everyday life happens where their errors sit near the fifteenth decimal place. Anything a person handles moves far slower than light, and its position is copied into the room by every photon and air molecule that leaves it, so often that the odds of it being anywhere else fall below one in 10⁷⁷. That copying makes a chair definite and its readings ordinary numbers. In 1960 Pound and Rebka measured how much slower a clock runs at the foot of a 22.5 meter tower, about 2.5 parts in a thousand million million. The best clock of the eighteenth century drifted by about one part in a million. Newton's absolute time was safe by a margin no mechanical clock could close. His mechanics is an accurate readout of a layer underneath, where the moving happens.

## Life, mind and choice

### What is life, physically?

Physically, life is a pattern that keeps itself the same at its own expense. In 1865 Claude Bernard described fasting rabbits that held the sugar and salt of their internal fluid steady while their livers took their bodies apart to do it. A stone persists for free, its readings sitting where they sit. Leave a rabbit alone for a week and its readings walk off, because holding them was work. The criterion is bounded homeostatic persistence, meaning maintenance under disturbance, paid for out of a finite budget. What a living thing defends is what it shows its neighbors, kept consistent with some possible world, while its interior travels a long way to keep it so. When the spending stops, every atom is where it was a minute earlier. The reading is what stops.

### Why do living things try to survive?

Living things try to survive because the patterns that pay to hold themselves together are the ones that remain, and nothing in physics wants anything. ### What makes a living thing freer than an atom?

History does. Freedom, if the word is to mean anything measurable, is room to be otherwise, and that room accumulates with every record a thing's past has written.
