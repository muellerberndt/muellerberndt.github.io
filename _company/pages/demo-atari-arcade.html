Atari Arcade.

Two brains are born when the page opens and learn to play while you watch.

Nothing to press. Each brain watches its teacher, takes the controls on its own and keeps learning from every reward. The first batches take the longest; a row says when its brain is deep in repair. Slow the games with the speed control to follow the play. Two emulators and two brains use four threads; on phones the second game starts on request.

Inside the arcade

From watching
to playing.

Each game runs on an Atari 2600 emulator in your browser. Its brain is born with a fixed seed: nine cortical columns over an 84 × 84 retina, a reflection ring, a policy ring and a value nucleus, 61,000 relations settling as one organ.

While watching, the teacher's moves are admitted in batches and a probe counts how often the brain already agrees. At 240 witnesses and 60 percent agreement the brain takes the controls. From then on every reward feeds the same brain through replayed value estimates; the numbers show the agreement, the repair effort falling, the sweeps per settle and the score curve with a badge from noob to legend.

The engine is a JavaScript port of the Cadence 0.50 reference engine, checked against the library on recorded fixtures. In recorded headless runs both brains reached expert: Freeway lived 17, 21, 16, 26, 21 and 21 against its teacher's 21; Atlantis 2000, 2000, 2000, 2000 and 2000 against 2000. Carnival and Space Invaders are left out because their brains degrade in self-play.
