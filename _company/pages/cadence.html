Three design patterns / One patch rule

Choose the brain
the task needs.

Each bounded, self-reading patch has local state, incoming ports, a prediction and records of experience. All three layouts settle by repairing prediction mismatches and use the same learning rule.

01

Flat settlement.

Practised response / Muscle memory

Think of a familiar movement becoming a direct response. Patches read sensory inputs directly and settle independently: an inexpensive starting point for simple prediction and control.

02

State-coupled settlement.

Coordination / Balance

Think of several parts adjusting together. Populations read other populations' live states, so learned intermediate representations can respond to one another during settlement.

03

Recursive observer settlement.

Reflective reasoning

Think of checking an interpretation against its mismatches. Observers read states and exact prediction errors; another observer can read those observers in the same settlement.

These are functional analogies, not biological equivalence or a ranking of intelligence. The three patterns can coexist in one brain.

Choosing a layout: capability and cost

All three are working design patterns in Cadence 0.50.0. Start with the least expensive layout that learns the behavior, then measure what extra connections add. State coupling and recursive error readback introduce richer interactions; their benefit and settling cost depend on the task. Measure query latency and learning cost separately.

What changes during settlement and learning?

For a query, weights and biases stay fixed while internal states settle. When Cadence admits an experience, supplied outcomes can constrain outputs and the relation parameters can change too, anchored to their values before that experience. All three patterns use that learning API; this illustration keeps parameters fixed.

State connections let a later constraint revise an earlier state. Recursive observation also brings exact prediction errors into that interaction. Returning arrows show influence through the objective's derivatives, not additional learned reverse edges. Changing the sensory signal retains activity; changing the layout resets it.

Observer depth, repair steps and prediction into the future are three different things.

The patch equations behind the illustration

predictionᵢ = tanh(biasᵢ + Σ weightᵢⱼ × signalⱼ)
errorᵢ = stateᵢ − predictionᵢ
objective = ½ Σ errorᵢ² + 0.18/2 × Σ stateᵢ²

A signal can be a supplied input, another patch's state or an observed prediction error. Predictions and errors are recomputed from the current state; errors cannot be adjusted independently to conceal disagreement.

The illustration uses bounded state updates, exact derivatives through the error readbacks and backtracking for objective decrease. It stops at its stationarity tolerance or a 240-step budget. The objective's numerical “energy” does not measure electrical energy.

The positive activity penalty matters. For the corresponding acyclic, unclamped model in the idealized zero-penalty limit, the stationary answer reduces to forward substitution. Positive penalties or output constraints allow a different compromise. That difference alone does not establish better task performance.
