Cadence / Demos

Brains that learn
while they run.

Watch brains learn live in your browser, evolve across generations and adapt to a body that changes. Every demo is working software from cadence-demos.

These brains are modeled on animal brains. System 1 learns, remembers and acts while it runs, with no training phase. System 2 adds optional observers that read the brain's own activity.

01 / Live learning in your browser

Atari Arcade.

Two brains born on page load.
Learning to play while you watch.

Each game runs on an Atari 2600 emulator in your browser. A newborn Cadence brain watches a teacher play, takes the controls on its own and keeps learning from the reward of its own actions. There is no training phase: the brain you watch is the brain that learns.

It is a System 1 brain, modeled on an animal brain: a retina, an association cortex with a working memory, a motor cortex, and a critic whose surprise acts like dopamine. Every action is the settled state of the whole brain.

In recorded runs both brains took the controls within two minutes of birth and played at their teacher's level.

Cadence 0.70 running in JavaScript, checked against the Python library. On phones the second game starts on request. The repository holds the measurements, their limits and a Python edition with four games.

02 / Learning and evolution in your browser

Patch World.

Brains that learn in one life
and evolve across generations.

A moving sun feeds a world with a fixed supply of mass. Every creature is born with untrained relations and learns in your browser tab from the consequences of its own actions. Every brain patch and settling sweep costs mass.

Brain layouts are inherited and mutate at each birth: width, depth, senses and optional observer stages. Energy and death decide which brains stay. Select a creature to watch its brain settle.

Enter the world ↗Source and recorded runs ↗

Within a life

Experience changes the brain.

There is no training phase. Each creature acts, sees what followed and repairs its own relations, for as long as it lives.

Across generations

Evolution changes the layout.

Offspring inherit a mutated brain plan and start with fresh relations. Observer stages that read prediction errors are an optional gene, absent in the founders.

A JavaScript implementation of the Cadence 0.70 population solver, checked against the library, with two published 6,000-tick runs preserving total mass. The experiment lets you inspect evolving brains; it does not establish an advantage from observers or depth.

03 / Run locally / For robot developers

Rover Lab.

Change the body.
The live brain adapts.

A simulated rover learns how its wheel commands turn into motion, from executed commands and odometry alone. Weaken its right wheel without telling it. The learning brain revises its forecasts within seconds of driving and keeps reaching its targets.

In a two-seed development screen on Cadence 0.70, learning Cadence reached 10/10 weakened-body targets, against 5/10 for a frozen copy of the same brain. The adaptive estimator also reached 10/10.

One mode

No training phase.

The same brain forecasts and learns on every control step. There is no switch between training and inference.

Measured consequences

It learns from what the body did.

Only executed commands and measured motion teach the brain. Wheel strength, the phase of the experiment and the change itself are hidden from it.

Control loop

Timing on the record.

Every step's sensing-to-command time is measured against a 100 ms deadline and misses are reported. A whole life saves to a checkpoint and resumes.

A four-patch body model inside a supplied heading controller, on the released cadence-net 0.70.0 package, beside an adaptive estimator and a small neural baseline. The viewer runs at best effort. This is a simulation of adaptation, not a physical robot result. Inspect the comparison ↗

04 / Play in your browser

AMEN.

A small learned brain.
A room full of jungle.

Press CUT A DUB. AMEN composes a 16- or 32-bar track from its trained record memory, then renders the drums and bass for playback. Adjust the variation and cut another.

The composer learned from 6.2 hours of music in 78 CPU minutes on a laptop. It keeps two kinds of memory, as brains do: slowly learned habits for the style and one-shot records of single moments.
