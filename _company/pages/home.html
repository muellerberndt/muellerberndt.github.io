The ambition

A robot should learn from what happens to it. Every changed payload. Every unfamiliar surface. Every correction.

Our goal is a brain that carries experience forward: adapting to its body, recovering when conditions change and becoming more useful with practice.

Cadence builds that intelligence from local predictive patches. Each holds state and records, reads through its connections and learns from experience. The task determines how those patches connect.

Three design patterns / One patch rule

A direct response.
A coordinated brain.
A view of its own errors.

Nature offers useful intuition: practised responses, coordination and reflective checking. Cadence gives each a concrete computational form.

01

Flat settlement.

Direct response

Patches read sensory inputs and settle their own states. A simple starting point for learning a response to the world.

02

State-coupled settlement.

Coordination

Populations also read one another's live states. Intermediate representations adjust together within a shared settlement.

03

Recursive observer settlement.

Internal feedback

Observers read states and exact prediction errors, including those of other observers. The brain's own activity becomes part of what it models.

These are functional analogies. All three patterns use the same settlement and learning rule; the useful connections depend on the task.
