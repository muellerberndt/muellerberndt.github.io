The difference

Most AI stops learning
before it ships.

Most of today's AI runs on transformers. A transformer is completely feed-forward, and in a typical deployed model its attention matrices and all other weights are fixed once training ends. What it meets in use does not change them.

A brain works differently. Its signals loop back on themselves, and it keeps learning for as long as it lives. Cadence is built the same way. It senses, decides and acts in real time, and every experience changes it a little.

That matters most for machines with bodies. A robot's world does not hold still.

Made for a body

What a robot gains.

A robot meets wear, new loads and places nobody trained it for. A brain that learns from experience can keep up with them.

On the job

It learns while it works.

The same brain acts and learns from the result of every action. There is no training phase before deployment.

Changed conditions

It adapts when things change.

A worn motor, a heavier load or a different floor shows up in what the robot senses. The brain adjusts from that, without being told what changed.

On the machine

It learns on the robot itself.

Each connection changes based on the activity next to it, as in a biological brain. Learning needs no round trip to a data centre.
