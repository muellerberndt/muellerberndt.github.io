Inside the tank

Measured wiring,
running.

A connectome is a wiring diagram measured in an animal: every cell, every synapse, how many contacts each connection carries. The compiler turns that table into a Cadence patch net without changing a number in it.

Every cell becomes a patch that holds one number. Every recorded connection becomes a relation weighted by its synapse count. Each patch keeps correcting its own number toward what its neighbours tell it until the whole net agrees, and that settled agreement is the answer the body reads. Push the net with a stimulus and the disagreement spreads and settles again: the gold flashes in the brain view are those local repairs.

The compilation

Five steps,
nothing invented.

  1. Read the release's own tables. Cells with their identity, type and position; synapses with sender, receiver and contact count. The files' digests go on the record.
  2. One patch per cell, one relation per connection. Each relation is weighted by its synapse count times the sender's declared sign. Nothing is added, moved or guessed; where the data lack a connection, the net lacks it too.
  3. Declare the unit and select the gain. The graded rate neuron and the time step are declared. The one free number, the gain, comes from a frozen protocol: scan from the least excitable gain upward, stop at the first ignition, keep the gain at which one training fact holds.
  4. Score the held-out facts once. On the measured wiring and on shuffled wirings that keep each cell's degrees, or also its input strengths, each with its own gain.
  5. Write the receipt and export. The receipt binds the source tables, the compiled net and the protocol by digest and discloses every exploratory look. The export feeds the browser engine, whose arithmetic matches the Cadence library to machine precision.

The compiler itself is not published. Everything it produced, the compiled nets and every receipt, ships with the demo, and the data behind them are the authors' public releases.

The larva

Every cell in the loop.

The three-day Platynereis dumerilii of Verasztó et al. 2025: 25,609 traced cells, 2,664 of them neurons, and every synapse onto its muscles and ciliated cells. The lamp lands on its real photoreceptors, a tap on its collar receptors, and its cilia and muscles are read from the real effector cells.

The fish

A brainstem that holds its gaze.

One side of a larval zebrafish hindbrain, 2,884 neurons and 75,195 synapses (Vishwanathan et al. 2024). After a saccade the compiled integrator holds the eyes on its own and lets them drift back over seconds, as the circuit does in the fish. The swim is a labelled pilot.

Declared, and checked

Nothing invented.

Signs, gain, the neuron's response law, the senses, the body and the time scale are declared on the pages. Each animal's net passes five of five held-out circuit checks that shuffled wirings fail, and the receipts are linked on the disclaimers page.

The nets are frozen: this demo compiles and runs measured wiring, it does not learn. The whole-fish release that exists today is a segmentation still being proofread by its community, and the disclaimers page says why it is not here.

The preprints

Why a settling net
is the target.

What a spiking network computes, once its rates have settled, is an equilibrium. A patch net rests exactly there, and reaches it without placing every spike.
