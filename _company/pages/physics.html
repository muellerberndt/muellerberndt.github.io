Observer Patch Holography

A world built from shared records.

Could a shared physical world emerge from bounded observers that compare their records and repair disagreement? Observer Patch Holography explores that question through mathematics and executable models.

The central idea

What a patch can observe.

An observer is a bounded, self-reading system. It has local state, ports or boundaries, records, and feedback that lets it repair disagreement.

Each patch has access to part of a larger system. Where patches overlap, their records must agree on shared meaning. The theory studies what follows when these local relationships support a consistent public account.

Finite normal-form theorems describe agreement and stability. Further constructions connect records to quantum probability on the stated event-algebra branch and, under specified response and transport hypotheses, the Standard Model gauge Lie algebra. The route to Einstein dynamics requires a common physical realization with the stated stress, entropy, continuum and scale data.

Mathematics and physical evidence.

Formal proofs establish consequences of specified assumptions; simulations test declared models. Establishing that this architecture describes nature requires physical realization and measurements that distinguish its predictions.
