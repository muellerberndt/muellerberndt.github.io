The difference

A frozen model cannot learn from the field.

Today's AI runs on transformers. A transformer is completely feed-forward, and its attention matrices are frozen once pretraining ends.

A robot in the field meets wear, new loads and new places. A frozen model learns from none of it. Cadence learns from each action and its result as it happens, the way an animal does.

Why a learning brain

Built like a brain. Made for a body.

Six things a robot gains from a brain that learns from experience.

On the job

It learns while it works.

The same brain acts and learns from the result of every action. There is no training phase before deployment.

Changed conditions

It adapts when things change.

A worn motor, a heavier load or a different floor shows up in what the robot senses. The brain adjusts from that, without being told what changed.

On the machine

It learns on the robot itself.

Each connection changes based on the activity next to it, as in a biological brain. Learning needs no round trip to a data centre.

Memory

It remembers.

A short-term memory carries the last few moments and a long-term memory keeps what mattered. Something experienced once can shape the next decision.

Settled answers

It does not act on a guess.

Cadence acts only once the whole brain has settled on an answer. When it cannot settle, it reports that, and the robot can fall back to a safe behaviour.

Inspection

You can see what it is doing.

The activity of every part of the brain can be read while it runs. The brain can also try out a response in imagination before the body commits to it.

Rover Lab / Working simulation

Change the body. Watch it adapt.

Weaken a simulated rover's wheel without telling it. The brain works out what changed from its own motion.

The rover learns how its wheel commands turn into motion from driving alone. When a wheel weakens, the live brain relearns its body within seconds and keeps reaching its targets. A frozen copy of the same brain falls behind.

Cadence is built from small self-reading patches. Each one holds its own state, listens to its neighbours, keeps records of experience and corrects itself through feedback. Together they settle on one answer, and experience changes how they are connected.
