The gap

Most AI stops learning before it ships.

Robots work in a world nobody trained them for. A model that is frozen at the factory cannot learn from any of it.

Transformers

Trained first, then deployed frozen.

Most of today's AI runs on transformers. A transformer is completely feed-forward, and in a typical deployed model its attention matrices and all other weights are fixed once training ends. New knowledge means fine-tuning or retraining, a separate run in a data centre.

Cadence

Running and learning at once.

Cadence follows the design of a brain. It senses, decides and acts in real time, and each action and its result changes it. The brain that works is the brain that learns.

Cadence is built from small self-reading patches. Each one holds its own state, listens to its neighbours, keeps records of experience and corrects itself through feedback. Together they settle on one answer, and experience changes how they are connected. For a robot this means learning on the job, adapting when its body changes and acting only on a settled answer.

The opportunity

We build the brains. Robot manufacturers build the bodies.

Robotics is our first market. The proposed product is a supported Cadence runtime and a brain prepared for the customer's embodiment, with a path from one useful integration to recurring fleet revenue.

A changed payload, a worn motor or a different work surface can turn a familiar action into a failed one. Our first customer test is whether a learning brain reduces the operator interventions that follow such a change.

01

Paid evaluation

Integrate one valuable task with a robot maker. Measure how the robot adapts against the maker's existing stack.

02

Production license

Convert useful results into a runtime and embodiment license, with deployment integration and support.

03

Fleet expansion

Expand through additional robots and tasks using the same brain architecture and reusable integration tools.
