The idea

Small models.
A shared answer.

Animal and human brains learn from experience and not by backpropagation with gradient descent. Cadence is designed the same way: its connections change locally while the brain keeps running.

Local predictive patches hold state and records, read through their connections, and repair their disagreement until the whole brain settles on one answer.

System 1 is the default: a continuing brain that learns from every outcome, remembers and acts. System 2 is optional: observers that read the brain's own activity and feed back into the same settlement.

Made for a body

What a robot gains.

A robot meets wear, new payloads and places nobody trained it for. A brain that learns from experience can keep up with them.

On the job

It learns while it works.

The same brain acts and learns from every measured outcome. There is no training phase before deployment and no difference between training and inference.

Changed conditions

It adapts when the body changes.

A worn actuator, a new payload or a different surface shows up in what the robot measures. The live brain adjusts from those measurements, without being told what changed.

Settled answers

It acts only on a settled answer.

Every action is the settled state of the whole brain. A brain that does not settle refuses to act, so a controller can fall back instead of executing a guess.

Learning you can measure.
