01 / THE STARTING POINT

Two views of one asset.
Keep the first question small.

Begin with ETH spot markets. Observe a centralized order book and an on-chain pool. Give the brain two paper execution ledgers with independent, prefunded balances. Test whether its evolving world model predicts consequences well enough to support a useful paired trade, further observation or no action.

A PRICE GAP IS NOT A PROFIT

Arbitrage is one possible behavior of the learned world model, with no hand-coded spread trigger. Fees, liquidity, latency and non-atomic fills can erase a price gap. ETH/WETH conversion and cross-venue custody also matter. Internal equilibrium does not prove market arbitrage.

THE CADENCE DIFFERENCE

The brain reads
its own state.

Cadence instantiates observer-like, self-reading systems: bounded patches with local state, ports, readback, records and feedback. Shared boundaries let those patches settle together.

Every cortex uses that same patch rule. World predictions and action proposals must come from settled activity. A copied input or feed-forward readout belongs among the comparison baselines.

02 / TWO CLOCKS, ONE LEARNER

Bootstrap in replay.
Meet reality in real time.

Recorded markets let evolution revisit experience faster. Live observation tests whether a selected brain can cope with the future it actually receives. Both belong in the design.

↠

FASTER EXPOSURE, THE SAME EVIDENCE

Speed up exposure.
Preserve market time.

Replay recorded events in causal order. Advance the simulator through the original timestamps, fill delays and reward delays. A month of market events can take less wall-clock time; execution does not become instantaneous.

Evolution sees training folds only. Chronological validation chooses candidates; sealed future windows test the locked procedure.

WITHIN A LIFE

The patch rule learns.

Matured outcomes teach the world model through the patch rule. Keep the genome fixed for each life so prediction, adaptation and attention can be measured separately.

ACROSS LIVES

The genome evolves.

A genetic algorithm explores wiring, patch budgets and candidate learning parameters. Hand-set choices stay as controls. Every candidate pays its compute and turnover costs.

AT PROMOTION

Evidence decides what runs.

Continuous learning does not guarantee continuous improvement. Keep a learning champion, its frozen twin and shadow challengers. Compare their outcomes; admit architecture changes through declared tests, with a path to rollback.

↺

When a sensory source is added, collect an overlapping history, bootstrap separate candidates, and compare with the same brain deprived of that source. More inputs earn their place through an ablation.

THE WORLD MODEL / A TESTABLE ATTENTION LOOP

Prediction gives surprise
a useful meaning.

The organizing hypothesis is a brain that models the market and its own actions. A price discrepancy, unexpected fill or inventory mismatch becomes something to investigate. Interest is an allocation of observation and computation; trading is a separate decision.

PREDICT → COMPARE → INVESTIGATE → DECIDE

What did the model expect?

For each candidate action, propose a settled prediction of the next market observation, execution result and portfolio state. When the relevant outcome matures, record the mismatch and teach the same local patch rule.

What deserves a closer look?

An attention control can route discrepancy readback to more observation or bounded private counterfactuals before a costed decision. A learned observer is an experimental alternative to that fixed control, with its own resource budget.

These are proposed market-model and attention interfaces. A conceptual diagram does not establish implementation or benefit.

SURPRISE IS EVIDENCE TO ASSESS

An anomaly can be noise.

A stale feed or random price jump can be surprising. Investigation must earn its cost; uncertainty can justify waiting, abstaining or repairing the data path.

VALUE AND SURPRISE DIFFER

A good trade may be familiar.

A correctly predicted arbitrage opportunity can be unsurprising and still useful. Prediction error cannot replace expected net value as the decision criterion.

ATTENTION NEEDS A CONTROL

Test the observer, then add depth.

Compare fixed attention, learned attention and no extra investigation with the same telemetry and compute. Add recursive layers only when they repair a measured failure of the shallower model.

04 / EVOLUTION WITH EXIT CRITERIA

Let the evidence
set the pace.

Complexity is a hypothesis. Expand only when a reproducible receipt answers the preceding question, and the required Cadence capability meets its own contract.

  1. 00

    Make the market replayable

    Record both feeds; validate timestamps, gaps, normalization and deterministic replay. Pin the pool and token identities; model independent inventories and paired execution.

    GATE Data & simulator receipt
  2. 01

    Set the bar with simple controls

    No trade from the same prefunded balances and a fee-aware spread rule establish the baseline. Separate arbitrage gains from the starting ETH inventory’s price changes. Give feed-forward and recurrent models the same information and resource budget.

    GATE Locked comparison protocol
  3. 02

    Evolve one small world model

    Test one-step predictions and decisions, fixed versus evolved wiring, and frozen versus continuing learning. Charge for investigation and compute; evaluate on unseen chronological windows.

    GATE Repeatable out-of-sample evidence
  4. 03

    Observe the live market

    Run paper decisions in real time. Compare actual arrival delays and price availability with replay assumptions. A live-capital step needs a separate decision and fixed risk limits.

    GATE Forward shadow receipt
  5. 04

    Add senses and specialized cortices

    Introduce one source at a time. Use the same patch rule in every cortex; compare against removing the added information and against a larger single brain.

    GATE Added value after added cost
  6. 05

    Test recursive self-observation

    Give observer patches readback of disagreement, uncertainty and resource use. Test whether coordination improves against equal-budget brains without that readback.

    GATE Controlled evidence of benefit

05 / GROWING WITH CADENCE

A stable experiment.
A replaceable brain implementation.

Keep the feed, market simulator and evaluator independent of the Cadence adapter. A capability becomes eligible through a versioned interface and checked evidence, rather than a roadmap date.
